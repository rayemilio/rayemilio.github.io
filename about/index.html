About

Drawn to systems, architecture, and the kind of problems that get more interesting the deeper you look.

Ray Reich is a musician, designer, educator, and software developer whose work explores how structure shapes experience.

His path began in music. A classical pianist from an early age, Ray was a finalist in the Carnegie Hall Concerto Competition at age twelve before going on to study at Berklee College of Music, where he earned a B.A. in Electronic Production and Design. While at Berklee, he developed a deep interest in synthesis, recording technology, and the emerging intersection of music and software—ultimately arriving at programming through sound.

Ray spent a formative period at MIT working with the Media Lab’s Opera of the Future group, contributing to experimental music technology, audio design, MIDI systems, and robotics-supported performance projects. During this time, he was also part of a team that became a finalist in a $100,000 international innovation competition.

Over the years, his work has crossed a wide range of disciplines. He has created audio for video games, supported live audiovisual systems for United Nations conferences, owned and operated a professional recording studio, served on nonprofit boards, taught hundreds of students—including many neurodiverse learners—and developed educational tools that make complex musical concepts easier to understand and visualize.

His creative work has received international recognition, including awards from the Venice Film Awards and the Rome Independent Prisma Awards for sound design.

Though the work spans music, software, education, typography, and design, the common thread is an interest in systems: how structure shapes understanding, behavior, and experience. The projects collected here are explorations of that idea from different directions.
