About

Drawn to systems, architecture, and the kind of problems that get more interesting the deeper you look.

Ray Reich is a musician, designer, educator, and software developer whose work explores how structure shapes experience.

A classical pianist from an early age, Ray was a finalist in the Carnegie Hall Concerto Competition at age 12 before going on to study at Berklee College of Music, where he earned a B.A. in Electronic Production and Design. While at Berklee, he developed a deep interest in synthesis, recording, and the intersection of music and technology.

Ray spent a formative period at MIT working with the Media Lab’s Opera of the Future group, contributing to experimental music technology, audio design, MIDI systems, and robotics-supported performance. During this time, he was also part of a team that became a finalist in a $100k Entrepreneurship Competition.

Over the years, his work has crossed a wide range of disciplines. He has created audio for video games, supported live AV systems at sensitive locations like the United Nations, owned and operated a recording studio, served on nonprofit boards, invited to speak at numerous schools and institutions, taught hundreds of students (including many neurodiverse learners), and developed curriculums and educational tools that make complex musical concepts easier to understand and visualize.

His creative work has received international recognition, including awards from the Venice Film Awards and the Rome Independent Prisma Awards for sound design.

Though his work spans music, software, education, and design, the common thread is an interest in systems: how structure shapes understanding, behavior, and experience.
