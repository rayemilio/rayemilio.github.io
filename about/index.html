About

Drawn to systems, architecture, and the kind of problems that get more interesting the deeper you look.
Designs tools and experiences that transform complexity into usability.

Ray Reich is a musician, designer, educator, and software developer whose work explores a central question: how can thoughtful structure shape the way we think, create, and interact?

That question first emerged through music. A classical pianist from an early age, Ray was a finalist in the Carnegie Hall Concerto Competition at 12 before studying Electronic Production and Design at Berklee College of Music, where he explored composition, synthesis, recording, and the relationship between technology and sound.

At MIT, he continued exploring that intersection with the Media Lab’s Opera of the Future group, working on experimental music technology, audio design, MIDI systems, and robotics-supported performance. During this period, he was also part of a team recognized as a finalist in the $100K Entrepreneurship Competition.

Across disciplines, Ray kept returning to the same underlying ideas: patterns, systems, interfaces, and the ways thoughtful design can make complexity understandable.

That curiosity has led him through a wide range of creative and technical work: designing audio for video games, developing curriculums and building educational tools, creating software, owning and operating a recording studio, supporting high-level technical environments including the United Nations, advising nonprofits, speaking at schools and institutions, and teaching hundreds of students including many neurodiverse learners.

His educational work focuses on making abstract ideas visible: transforming complicated musical concepts into tools, systems, and experiences that feel intuitive.

His creative projects have received international recognition, including awards from the Venice Film Awards and Rome Independent Prisma Awards for sound design.

Whether through music, software, education, or design, the thread remains: a deep interest in how structure shapes understanding, behavior, and experience, and a pursuit of systems that create clarity, invite understanding, and feel inevitable.
